A playground, not a prediction.

Real incompressible flow equations, an intentionally simplified world. SI properties inform the comparison; the visualization uses a normalized, viscosity-compressed model, not a physically matched Reynolds number. Water is a fully submerged, single-phase flow.

How this model works

Inside the solver

A staggered finite-difference grid evolves velocity with semi-Lagrangian advection, implicit viscous diffusion and iterative pressure projection. Passive dye and optional tracers follow that computed field. Dragging adds a local velocity impulse and dye; “Stir flow” provides a keyboard alternative.

What the numbers mean

Re and ν use the displayed SI density, viscosity, speed U and independent reference length L. Inlet speed maps to 0.35 + 0.16U normalized units; display viscosity is 0.000035 + 0.00032 log₁₀(1 + ν / 10⁻⁶). This logarithmic mapping preserves viscosity ordering, not physical similarity. The simulation clock is normalized, not real elapsed flow time.

No free surface

No waves, splashes, buoyancy, cavitation, compressibility or multiphase mixing. Walls and the grid-aligned obstacle are impermeable with approximate free-slip boundaries, not a resolved no-slip boundary layer. A coarse 2D grid and numerical diffusion cannot predict turbulence, drag or lift. Changing media or geometry restarts the flow. L only changes the reported SI metrics.
